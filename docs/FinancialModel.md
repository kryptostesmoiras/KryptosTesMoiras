# Financial Model — Kryptos Tēs Moiras

## 1. Purpose

Kryptos Tēs Moiras is designed to operate as a self-sustaining digital ecosystem. Its financial model is intended to cover infrastructure, hosting, maintenance, security, domain costs, and continued development through revenue generated by the platform and voluntary donations.

The platform's funds are intended to support its operation and long-term continuity rather than to serve as a mechanism for distributing profits to its founder.

## 2. Funding Sources

### 2.1 Voluntary Donations

Donations are voluntary contributions intended to support the platform's operation and development. A donation is not a payment for a specific product or service unless the terms presented for that contribution explicitly state otherwise.

### 2.2 Commercial Application Royalties

Kryptos Tēs Moiras may distribute commercial applications under agreements that define the applicable royalty or commission.

Each agreement should specify, as applicable:

- the royalty rate or calculation method;
- the amount on which the royalty is calculated;
- the payment schedule;
- how refunds, chargebacks, taxes, and payment-processing fees are treated;
- reporting and reconciliation procedures;
- the conditions for changing or ending the agreement.

The applicable agreement governs each distribution. This document does not establish a universal royalty rate.

### 2.3 Domain Registration and Management

Domain-related services may be offered through a paid subscription. The service description should specify what is included, such as registration, renewal, DNS management, or other domain-related features, and identify any third-party fees or limitations.

### 2.4 Other Services

Additional services may be introduced if they support the platform's purpose and are described in their applicable terms.

AI functionality is intended to remain available without a paid AI subscription from Kryptos Tēs Moiras. Users may optionally configure a provider or model of their choice where supported. Charges imposed by an external AI provider remain separate from Kryptos Tēs Moiras unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## 3. Allocation of Platform Funds

Platform funds may be allocated to the following operational categories:

1. **Operating expenses** — hosting, electricity, connectivity, storage, backups, and other infrastructure costs.
2. **Maintenance and security** — updates, monitoring, incident response, audits, and recovery capabilities.
3. **Domain and service costs** — domain registrations, renewals, certificates, and required third-party services.
4. **Continuity reserve** — funds retained for unexpected expenses, outages, equipment replacement, or service continuity.
5. **Development reserve** — resources for improvements, new features, and capacity expansion.
6. **Treasury placements** — where selected, funds belonging to the platform and not required for near-term obligations may be placed under a defined treasury policy.

These are planning categories. Accounting treatment and the ownership of funds must reflect the actual operating arrangement.

## 4. Treasury and Restricted Funds

The platform may maintain a central treasury wallet or account for platform-owned funds. Internal accounting records may track the origin, allocation, and intended use of funds.

Funds should be distinguished at least as follows:

- **Available operating funds:** accessible for ordinary expenses.
- **Continuity reserves:** retained for emergencies and planned liabilities.
- **Restricted or locked placements:** funds committed for a defined term and unavailable until maturity, subject to the product's terms.
- **Amounts payable to third parties:** royalties, refunds, or other obligations not yet settled.

Funds committed to a locked placement should not be counted as immediately available operating liquidity. Before making a placement, the operator should account for expected expenses, payment obligations, maturity dates, fees, counterparty risk, and the possibility of loss. Historical or advertised yields are not guarantees of future performance or capital preservation.

Only funds that are genuinely available to and owned by the platform should be considered for the platform's own treasury placements. Internal balances representing funds held for users or amounts owed to developers must be accounted for separately and must not be treated as unrestricted platform treasury merely because they are recorded in a central wallet.

## 5. Internal Wallet and Accounting Model

The system may create an internal wallet record for each user account. These records define separate accounting and authorization scopes; their existence does not by itself mean that each record corresponds to a separate on-chain wallet.

The internal ledger should record, as applicable:

- the account and transaction identifiers;
- the transaction type and timestamp;
- the amount and denomination;
- the source and destination accounting entries;
- the purpose, such as subscription payment, royalty, refund, or adjustment;
- the transaction status and reconciliation result;
- the authorization or agreement supporting the transaction.

Ledger entries should be auditable and protected against unauthorized alteration. Corrections should be recorded as traceable adjustments rather than by silently overwriting historical entries.

The ledger should distinguish platform revenue from amounts owed to developers, refunds due, user balances, and other liabilities. The system should reconcile internal records against the actual balances and transactions of the relevant wallets or payment providers.

## 6. Commercial Distribution and Royalties

Commercial application distribution should be governed by explicit terms accepted by the relevant parties. Those terms should define the commercial model for each application or distribution channel.

A royalty calculation should be reproducible from transaction records. Reports should identify the relevant period, eligible transactions, refunds and adjustments, fees where applicable, the calculation method, and the resulting amount payable.

The platform should not mark a developer as paid until the payment has been confirmed by the applicable payment system or network.

## 7. AI Access and External Providers

AI functionality is intended to remain available without a paid AI subscription from Kryptos Tēs Moiras.

Where supported, users may choose to configure their own external AI provider or use a local model. The interface should clearly distinguish:

- processing performed by Kryptos Tēs Moiras infrastructure;
- processing performed by a user-selected external provider;
- costs charged directly by that provider;
- data that may be sent to that provider.

API credentials must be protected as secrets and must not be exposed in source repositories, ordinary logs, or user-visible error messages. Users should be informed that choosing an external provider may cause data to be processed under that provider's terms and privacy practices.

## 8. Controls and Reconciliation

The financial system should implement controls appropriate to the scale and risk of the platform, including:

- access controls and least-privilege permissions;
- strong authentication for privileged treasury operations;
- separation between routine service processes and treasury authorization;
- transaction limits and explicit approval for sensitive operations;
- periodic reconciliation of internal ledgers with external wallet or provider records;
- monitoring for duplicate, inconsistent, or unauthorized transactions;
- protected, auditable records of financial operations;
- documented backup and recovery procedures.

A central wallet should not be treated as sufficient evidence that all internally recorded balances are covered. The platform should be able to reconcile the aggregate ledger with actual assets and separately identify amounts owed to users or third parties.

## 9. Sustainability Principle

The platform's financial objective is operational self-sufficiency. Revenue and donations are intended to fund the services and reserves required to maintain and improve Kryptos Tēs Moiras over time.

The platform should prioritize reliable operations, transparent commercial terms, responsible treasury management, and continuity of service over speculative returns.

## 10. Scope and Status

This document describes the intended financial and accounting model. It is not a promise of a particular yield, a guarantee of funds, or a substitute for applicable service terms and commercial agreements.

The implemented system, its accounting records, and its public statements should accurately reflect the actual ownership, custody, and movement of funds.
